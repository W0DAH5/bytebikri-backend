# Entry-Phase Ads Playbook — What Actually Works

**Project:** bytebikri · **Date:** 2026-09-21 · **Status:** Decision-ready
**Companion to:** [`monetization-model.md`](./monetization-model.md)

You asked two things: *(1) is there a company/program that lets this work at entry phase, and (2) if not,
design the fallback.* **The answer to (1) is yes — and it is a Google product built for exactly this.**
The fallback in (2) turns out to be the same architecture, so you build it either way.

---

## 1. The headline: **AdSense for Platforms (AFP) Transparent**

You described: *"users host ads on their own content, the ad company signs them up with their own ID, and
the revenue never passes through me but I still get a share."*

Google's AFP documentation, almost word for word:

> "Platforms integrating with AdSense via AFP can implement a flexible revenue share between themselves and
> their users. **Ad revenue payments post-revenue share are facilitated directly to each party by Google.**"

> "**Embedded Sign Up** — Allow users who are new to AdSense to sign up for new accounts **completely within
> the CMS and site building platform**. Removes friction and eases onboarding."

> "AdSense APIs designed to make signing up with AdSense feel **more native to your users**, with less back
> and forth between the platform UI and AdSense."

> "**Secure Payments** — Ad revenue generated by both the child publishers and platforms are paid
> **directly on Net-22** in multiple currencies. Both parties can select from multiple payment methods."

> "In the past, AdSense offered a product called AdSense Host API which enabled 3-way revenue sharing. AFP
> is similar to the Host API, however with AFP, **the AdSense account belongs to the child publisher and the
> platform doesn't need to undertake any account management tasks.**"

Sources: [AFP home](https://developers.google.com/adsense/platforms) ·
[Transparent overview](https://developers.google.com/adsense/platforms/transparent/overview)

**Every one of your constraints is satisfied by a shipping product.** Not an approximation — this is the
literal feature set you were describing from first principles.

### Your four questions, answered

| You asked | AFP answer |
|---|---|
| Can an ad company sign users up *from inside my app*, with the user's own ID? | **Yes.** "Embedded Sign Up" and "Speedy signup" via the AdSense Management API |
| Can revenue bypass me entirely? | **Yes.** Google pays each party directly, post-revenue-share, Net-22 |
| Can I still take a cut? | **Yes.** You configure 0–100% for you and 0–100% for the user |
| Do I have to manage their accounts? | **No.** "The platform doesn't need to undertake any account management tasks" |

### And it fixes the problem I flagged last time

I told you earlier that because the ad account belongs to the user, **you'd never see their earnings** —
so no in-app money dashboard without a per-user OAuth dance. AFP removes that:

> "**Transparent Reporting** — Pull data and reporting at the platform level and down to the child publisher
> level within our reporting UI or through our API."

And it fixes the other one too. I warned that the ads.txt file would need thousands of lines:

> "given that most AFP platform customers have **thousands of child accounts**, managing this file and even
> Google and other bidders' ability to process it, becomes difficult."

Their recommended fix (Option 2): put `data-ad-host` on every ad tag — or use the **platform account meta
tag** — and then **"the ads.txt file will only ever need one entry."** One line. Forever. That's the
problem gone.

**This is the thing you were looking for.**

## 2. The catch: it's invite-only, and it's enterprise

> "AdSense for Platforms is currently invite-only. To register your interest, please submit the form below."
> — [Register interest](https://developers.google.com/adsense/platforms/register-interest)

What onboarding actually looks like, per [the getting-started guide](https://developers.google.com/adsense/platforms/transparent/initial-tasks):

1. **Sign your contract** — "Your Account Manager will share an AFP contract with you... an amendment to the
   AdSense terms and conditions."
2. **Set up your AdSense account** — you need your own; they adjust it for AFP.
3. **Choose your initial configuration** and hand it to Google through your Account Manager.
4. You get **dedicated technical support from the AdSense engineering team** during integration, then a
   **dedicated Account Manager** afterwards.

So: a named human, a contract, a sales cycle. **Not a traffic threshold** — that's the crucial difference
from MCM's 90M–200M impressions. But it is a business-development relationship, and AFP's stated customers
are content platforms and CMS/site-builders (Wix/Shopify-blog class companies) that already have traffic.

**Honest read on your odds today:** applying cold as a pre-launch project with an empty repo will most
likely get you "come back when you have traffic." That is not a reason to skip it — the sales cycle is long,
so **register interest early, with a real story and a demo**, while you build on the interim path below.
Same architecture either way, so nothing is wasted.

Also note: **AFP is AdSense, which is web.** Not native mobile. Answering your "web mobile whatever is
possible" — **build web.** That's where every option in this document lives.

## 3. The important side-finding: AFP **Direct**

AFP has a second mode, and it's a different business model. Worth understanding before you commit.

> "AFP **Direct** lets you monetize your platform's content including user generated content and sites run
> by your users. **Sub-AdSense accounts are intended to be created for each of your users**, allowing you to
> isolate traffic from one user into one sub-account. This allows AdSense to **localize spam and policy
> enforcement to the sub-account, keeping your main platform account in good health and standing** from a
> policy perspective, as Google does not serve ads for users who don't pass checks."
> — [Direct overview](https://developers.google.com/adsense/platforms/direct/overview)

Also: *"In exchange, the platform collects 100% of the ad revenue"* — users get the service free.

**This directly solves the single biggest risk I raised earlier.** Remember: I warned that one user posting
prohibited content can put *your whole domain* and *every other user's account* at risk, because Google
notifies all publishers on a violating site. AFP Direct **isolates enforcement per user account.** One bad
actor gets localized; your platform stays healthy.

Read the two side by side:

| | **AFP Transparent** | **AFP Direct** |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns the AdSense account | Each **user** | Sub-accounts Google creates **for** your users |
| Who gets paid | **Both** — Google pays you and the user directly | **You** — 100% of ad revenue |
| What users get | Their own earnings | The service, free |
| Your cut | 0–100%, your choice | 100% |
| Policy risk isolation | Shared on your domain | **Isolated per user sub-account** |
| Matches what you described | ✅ **Yes** | ✗ |

**Your model is Transparent.** But the *policy isolation* feature of Direct is genuinely valuable, and
Transparent users each having their own AdSense account gives you partial isolation anyway — a user's own
account absorbs their own ban. Worth raising with the Account Manager whether the isolation behaviour also
applies in Transparent.

**There is also a real strategic question here:** Direct means you keep all ad money but users earn nothing
from ads (they earn from selling assets instead). Given I already showed you Tier-3 CPMs are thin, **Direct
may actually be the better business** — you capture the ad value, users focus on selling digital assets, and
you avoid the whole "why are my earnings so low" support burden. Keep it on the table.

## 4. The fallback — and why you build it anyway

**Good news: the fallback and the AFP target are the same architecture.** If AFP never happens, or while you
wait, you run this:

### 4a. Web + plain AdSense, multiple publishers on one domain

Google explicitly permits it: *"Publishers are allowed to place ad code from more than one AdSense account on
a page that complies with our program policies"* ([Google](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/115979)).
Your root domain is approved once; users add your domain, get their own publisher ID, and you inject their tag
on their pages.

Workable for a handful of early users. Degrades as you scale: shared ad-density ceiling, thousands of
ads.txt lines, and **shared policy liability** (the §3 problem).

### 4b. The subdomain model — your best interim bet

This is the structurally important one, and it maps exactly onto your "land lease" metaphor.

Give each user their own space: `alice.bytebikri.com`, `bob.bytebikri.com`. Now, as far as the ad network is
concerned, **each user has their own site** — which is a thing every ad network already supports.

How the ads.txt side works, per [Google's ads.txt FAQ](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9785052):

> "Google crawls and enforces ads.txt files placed on subdomains, where one exists, and is **referenced from
> the ads.txt file on the root domain**. To reference a subdomain in your root domain ads.txt file, you add a
> `subdomain=` declaration... **You only need to do this if the authorized seller or your publisher ID are
> different for the subdomain** when compared to the root domain."

So your root `bytebikri.com/ads.txt` reads:

```
google.com, pub-YOURPLATFORMID, DIRECT, f08c47fec0942fa0
subdomain=alice.bytebikri.com
subdomain=bob.bytebikri.com
```

…and each subdomain serves its own `ads.txt` with its owner's publisher ID. And this is *precisely*AFP
Option 1: *"the ads.txt files have to be posted on each subdomain... and the ads.txt file has to contain the
child publisher ID... based on the value of the `data-ad-client` parameter."* The only difference is that
AFP formally whitelists your domain so it scales to thousands instead of being crawled per-subdomain.

**Why this matters:** it converts your hard problem ("many publishers on one domain") into the easy,
universally-supported one ("each publisher has their own site"). Your platform domain becomes a *host*, and
each user is a real, independent publisher.

**One caveat, stated plainly:** AdSense removed the ability to *add subdomains* to an account back in
March 2023 — subdomain handling has been in flux and the sources disagree on edge cases. The `subdomain=`
referral mechanism is documented, but **validate it with your own account and ideally get it in writing from
your Account Manager before you architect the whole platform on it.** I am reasonably confident this works;
I am not willing to tell you it's guaranteed. Treat AFP as the thing that makes it officially supported.

### 4c. Alternative ad partners (if Google says no)

Ranked by fit for "platform with many small publishers":

| Partner | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Google Certified Publishing Partner (GCPP)** — Snigel, Setupad, PubPower, Publift, Clickio, AdPushup, Mile, Newor Media | These *are* the companies that hold MCM/AdX access. A GCPP can be your parent and handle the multi-publisher plumbing commercially. **Most realistic non-Google path.** Clickio and Ezoic-style platforms work from ~100K pageviews/month |
| **Ezoic** | No traffic minimum, AI optimisation, works with subdomains. Mid-tier approval difficulty |
| **MGID / Taboola / Primis / Connatix** | Native and video widgets — good for UGC because widgets are per-placement and less tied to domain-level ads.txt. MGID works from ~3–5K visitors/day |
| **Media.net, Sovrn, Infolinks, Adsterra, PropellerAds** | Low/no minimums; lower quality and RPM; usable as a supplement while you grow |
| **Affiliate networks** (Amazon Associates, Cuelinks, EarnKaro, Sovrn Commerce) | **Underrated here.** A per-user affiliate tag is *trivially* per-user — no ads.txt, no domain problem, no policy collision. Users monetise contextually and you can take a cut by agreement |

**The affiliate angle deserves real attention.** Everything that makes ad networks awkward — domain-level
authorisation, shared ads.txt, shared policy liability — simply does not exist for affiliate links. A user
pastes their own affiliate ID, it's their account, they get paid directly by the merchant, and you take a cut
by agreement. For a "mixed digital assets" marketplace where users review and recommend things, this maps far
better than display ads. Keep it in the mix.

## 5. The decisions AFP will ask you for

When you apply, Google asks for exactly four configuration choices. You should decide these now, because
they determine your data model.

| Question | Your options | My recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Domain** | Your platform domain | `bytebikri.com` |
| **Domain structure** | Custom domains, **subdomains**, **subfolders**, URLs | **Subdomains** — clean per-user isolation, and it's the shape both AFP's ads.txt options assume |
| **Revenue share** | 0–100% each, applied *after* the standard AdSense revenue share | ✅ **DECIDED: start at 0% for you** through launch (users keep 100%) to attract supply, then move to **10–20%** once you're delivering real discovery and traffic. **Read §5a before you commit to this publicly** |
| **Ad settings control** | Parent (platform) account, or child (user) accounts | **Platform/parent** — dramatically simpler. Ad settings come from one place, you control blocking and compliance, users can't misconfigure themselves into a policy strike |
| **Onboarding flow** | Copy-paste publisher ID (API-less) or semi-assisted API flow | Start **API-less** (ship faster), move to **API flow** for embedded signup once accepted |

That revenue-share choice is a real strategic decision: **taking 0% at launch is not charity, it's
customer acquisition.** Supply of good creators is your bottleneck, and "you keep 100% of your ad revenue"
is the single strongest pitch you have against every competitor.

### 5a. DECIDED: Transparent at 0%, cut later — and the trap in that

**Decision:** AFP Transparent, platform revenue share **0%** through launch, with the intent to introduce a
cut once bytebikri is delivering real discovery and traffic.

This is the right call. But "free now, charge later" has a specific failure mode, and it is worth designing
around *before* you write the marketing copy.

**The trap.** If you launch saying "keep 100% of your ad revenue, forever," then move to 20% a year later, you
have not changed a business model — you have **broken a promise**. Expect a creator backlash, a wave of
churn, and a permanent trust discount on everything you say afterwards. The number is small; the betrayal is
not.

**Three rules to defuse it:**

1. **Say it out loud from day one.** "Free while we're in beta. Once we're sending you meaningful traffic,
   we'll take a modest share — here's the schedule." A published schedule is a promise you *can* keep.
2. **Tie the cut to something they can see.** Trigger it on a metric, not a date: *"we take 0% until you've
   received Rs X from us through ads, or until the platform sends Y visitors to your space."* A cut that
   arrives when the money is visibly working is a fee. A cut that arrives on a calendar is a tax.
3. **Put it in the ToS now, with a cap.** "Platform share will not exceed X% and will change only with 90
   days' notice." Grandfather early adopters — they took the risk, they keep the 0%.

**The bigger question: should the cut be on ads at all?**

Look at the arithmetic. I already showed you Tier-3 CPMs are thin — a user might earn a few dollars per
10,000 views. **20% of a few dollars is cents.** You'd be spending your scarcest resource (creator trust) to
collect a rounding error.

Compare the alternative: a **5–10% cut on digital-asset sales**, where one template sale can exceed a month
of impressions. Same user, same relationship, far more revenue per point of goodwill — and it is *commerce*,
which fits cleanly with a merchant-of-record, rather than ad-revenue share, which drags in the AFP
relationship, reporting reconciliation, and the trust problem above.

**Recommendation: keep the ad rev share at 0% permanently and monetise the sale instead.** Ads then become
the free layer that keeps content flowing and gives you a reason to be in the user's dashboard daily — and
the cut lands where the actual money is. This also unlocks the strongest possible pitch: *"your ad revenue is
100% yours, forever. We make money when you sell something."* That is a genuinely differentiated position,
and it is the truth, so you never have to walk it back.

early with a working product to show.

## 7. What to build now

Because the interim path and AFP share a shape, build this and you're ready for either:

**Data model**
- `users`, `spaces` (the subdomain/subfolder — your "land"), `ad_identities` (publisher ID, host ID, status,
  provider, verified_at), `ad_placements`, `impressions_served`, `ad_events`.
- Per-user status machine: `unlinked → pending → active → restricted → revoked`.
- **Store the publisher ID and the host ID as separate fields.** AFP's `data-ad-client` (child) and
  `data-ad-host` (platform) are different values and the `data-ad-host` route is what collapses ads.txt to
  one line. Design for both from day one.

**Serving**
- Resolve `content → owner → ad identity → tag`, server-side, cached.
- Emit `data-ad-client` **and** `data-ad-host` on every tag, plus the **platform account meta tag**.
- Never serve a restricted/revoked identity. Fall back to house or your own ads.
- One ads.txt strategy, switchable: dynamic root file (interim) ↔ single-entry + host ID (AFP).

**Admin**
- Kill switch with audit trail; moderation queue; per-user ad health view.
- Platform-level reporting ingest (AFP API later; your own impression logs now).

**Compliance**
- Google-certified CMP + IAB TCF v2.3 + Consent Mode v2 — required for EEA/UK/CH regardless of AFP.
- ToS: ad placement permission, content warranties, no earnings guarantee, removal rights.

**Do NOT build**
- Payout rails, KYC, withholding, invoicing for users. Both the interim model and AFP route money around you
  by design. That's the whole point. Don't rebuild what Google sells you.
